Executive Summary

Corporate secretarial in Romania is the practical governance and legal-administration function through which a company maintains its formal existence, reliable decision trail and current legal visibility. It extends beyond incorporation to the continuing management of constitutional records, corporate approvals, representation, registered office, capital and other particulars recorded in the Trade Register.

In practice, the function combines internal company record control with outward coordination through the National Trade Register Office, known by its Romanian abbreviation ONRC. Corporate events are documented through shareholder and management decisions, then assessed for registration, publication, notification or information-update consequences.

The legal framework is shaped principally by Companies Law No. 31/1990, the legislation governing the Trade Register, accounting and reporting requirements, and beneficial-ownership rules. The precise requirements depend on the entity type, the event concerned and the company’s ownership and operating profile.

Cross-border relevance is substantial where Romanian companies are held by foreign groups, used in regional structures or subject to international financing and diligence. Foreign parent instructions must be translated into Romanian corporate acts, registration filings, appropriate signature arrangements and locally defensible company records.

Object Definition

Corporate secretarial in Romania is the professional function concerned with maintaining the formal corporate and administrative life of Romanian entities. It coordinates the documentary, governance and registry-facing work needed to keep an entity’s internal records and external registered position coherent over time.

DefinitionThe professional governance and legal administration function concerned with maintaining the formal corporate life of Romanian entities, including company records, shareholder and management documentation, ONRC registrations, statutory update coordination, beneficial ownership compliance and recurring company maintenance.

Scope

The Registry Object covers the ongoing operational discipline through which a Romanian company is kept formally organised, properly documented and aligned with its Trade Register profile. It treats corporate secretarial work as a continuing governance function rather than a single incorporation service.

Covered MattersConstitutional document maintenance, shareholder decisions, management and director changes, representation and signatory arrangements, registered-office changes, capital events, ONRC filing coordination, Trade Register extracts, beneficial ownership declarations, annual reporting coordination and company-record maintenance.
Functional BoundaryThe Registry Object concerns governance order, statutory administration and corporate continuity. It explains the function that connects internal decisions with register-facing and compliance-facing consequences.
Related but Not PrimaryTax structuring, labour law, bookkeeping, litigation, regulated-sector compliance, transactional negotiation and broader legal advisory may interact with corporate secretarial work but are not the primary object.
Outside ScopeGeneral commercial consulting, sales activity, promotional services and non-governance operational management without a company-law or statutory-maintenance connection.

Purpose

The purpose of corporate secretarial work in Romania is to preserve the legal and administrative integrity of an entity throughout its lifecycle. It ensures that corporate decisions, company records and Trade Register information remain accurate, timely and capable of supporting interaction with authorities, banks, auditors, counterparties, investors and group stakeholders.

Purpose StatementTo ensure that Romanian entities maintain accurate governance documentation, current Trade Register particulars, appropriate beneficial ownership information and a coherent record of formal corporate acts.
Practical RationaleTo enable incorporation, operational continuity, change management, annual maintenance, financing, diligence and restructuring through a reliable corporate record and registry position.

Primary Outcome

The intended outcome is a Romanian entity whose internal corporate file and external legal profile are mutually consistent. The entity should be able to evidence its governance, authority, ownership-related declarations and registered particulars when scrutiny or a transaction requires it.

OutcomeA Romanian company with current ONRC data, orderly constitutional and decision records, appropriate beneficial ownership documentation, coordinated reporting and a clear audit and due-diligence trail.

Country Characteristics

Romanian corporate secretarial work is shaped by a Trade Register-centred environment. ONRC and its territorial offices provide the operational infrastructure through which incorporation and many company changes are registered, while formal company documentation provides the basis for the outward filing position.

Operational CultureFormal documentation, registry alignment and procedural completeness are central. Corporate actions should be translated into an organised local record before or alongside registration action.
Registry EnvironmentThe National Trade Register Office administers the Romanian Trade Register through a central and territorial structure, with electronic services and public-information functions.
Document FormalityThe nature of the corporate event determines the supporting documents, approvals, signatures, translations and procedural steps required for valid implementation.
Language ExpectationRomanian is central to domestic registry practice. Foreign group documents may require appropriate Romanian translation and adaptation to local corporate-law form.
Commercial ContextRomanian entities are frequently used by regional and international groups, which creates a recurring need to bridge group governance with local registration and documentation requirements.

Process Flow

Corporate secretarial work normally follows a sequence from identifying the company and its current register position to documenting the event, assessing formal consequences and maintaining a reliable record after implementation. The sequence helps prevent divergence between group intentions, company decisions and ONRC data.

1. Entity MappingIdentify the Romanian entity type, Trade Register number, registered office, ownership profile, management structure and present ONRC position.
2. Record ReviewReview the constitutive act, previous shareholder and management decisions, powers, register extracts, beneficial ownership information and reporting position.
3. Trigger IdentificationDetermine whether the matter concerns formation, management, representation, office, ownership, capital, business object, annual maintenance, restructuring or transaction readiness.
4. Governance DocumentationPrepare or organise resolutions, decisions, meeting materials, amended constitutional documents, mandates, declarations and supporting evidence.
5. Registry and Compliance AssessmentDetermine whether ONRC filing, publication, beneficial ownership action, tax coordination, reporting action or other notification is required.
6. Filing and Record UpdateSubmit required documentation through the appropriate channel and update the internal company file once the event is implemented.
7. Maintenance and Diligence ReadinessPreserve approvals, filing evidence, extracts, translated documents and calendar follow-up so the company remains ready for audit, banking or transaction review.

Decision Tree

The decision tree identifies the threshold questions that commonly determine the correct corporate secretarial route. It should be used as an operational framework rather than as a substitute for case-specific Romanian legal advice.

  1. Identify the Romanian legal entity and the business event that has occurred or is proposed.
  2. Confirm whether the event affects the constitutive act, shareholders, management, representation, registered office, capital, ownership profile or another formal company matter.
  3. Determine which corporate body must approve the event and which local documents must evidence that approval.
  4. Assess whether the event requires registration, publication, beneficial ownership action, tax coordination or annual-reporting action.
  5. Confirm documentary, signature, translation, formality and timing requirements before filing.
  6. Reconcile the final internal company record with the updated external registry position and retain evidence.

Romanian entities frequently operate within foreign-owned or multinational structures. Corporate secretarial work therefore often translates group decisions and English-language governance materials into an execution path that satisfies Romanian company-law, registry, language and documentary expectations.

RecognitionRomanian entities can form part of EU and wider international group structures, making reliable local corporate data and document control important to external stakeholders.
Foreign CompaniesForeign parents need to distinguish group approvals from the local Romanian decisions, filings and documentary form required to implement them.

Operating Constraints & Risks

The primary operational risk is misalignment: a business event may be agreed commercially but insufficiently documented, incompletely filed or not reflected in the company’s retained records. A disciplined process reduces the risk of defective authority, registry inconsistency and transaction delay.

Record Integrity RiskInternal company documents may not reflect the entity’s actual ownership, management or authority position if maintenance is delayed.
Registry Consistency RiskTrade Register data can diverge from internal governance records where changes are not identified and coordinated promptly.
Documentary Formality RiskIncomplete approvals, inappropriate signing arrangements, missing evidence or inadequate translations can delay or undermine implementation.
Beneficial Ownership RiskOwnership changes may create declaration and record-maintenance obligations that require careful analysis.
Timing RiskLate filings, annual maintenance delays or slow cross-border document circulation can create compliance and transaction friction.
Cross-Border Coordination RiskForeign instructions can fail to address local legal form, language, filing and evidence requirements.

FAQ

The following questions address common starting points. They provide orientation only; the applicable requirements depend on the entity, legal event and current Romanian law and procedure.

Is Corporate Secretarial Work in Romania Limited to Incorporation?No. It continues through management and representation changes, amendments, ownership and capital events, annual reporting coordination, record maintenance and transaction preparation.
Which Authority Is Central to Company Registration in Romania?The National Trade Register Office (ONRC) is central to the Romanian Trade Register and to many incorporation and company-change filings.
Are Romanian Company Records Relevant to Foreign Parent Companies?Yes. Foreign parents need local company records, approvals and registry data that support group control, reporting, banking, audit and diligence requirements.
Can a Parent Company Decision Replace Local Romanian Documentation?Not necessarily. Group decisions often need to be translated into appropriate Romanian corporate acts, supporting documents and, where applicable, Trade Register filings.
When Does Beneficial Ownership Require Attention?Beneficial ownership should be assessed at incorporation and when relevant ownership or control changes occur, taking account of the entity’s current obligations and available exemptions.
Are Trade Register Details Sufficient for Due Diligence?No. Register data is important, but diligence commonly also requires constitutional documents, corporate decisions, ownership evidence, authority records, filing evidence and financial-reporting materials.
